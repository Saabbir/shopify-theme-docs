## The full submission timeline, realistically

Publishing to the Theme Store is not a same-day process. A rough sense of timeline, so you can plan the rest of the project around it:

| Stage | Typical time investment |
|---|---|
| Partner Dashboard + store setup (if not already done) | A day, mostly waiting on account verification |
| Packaging and submitting | A few hours, once the theme itself is genuinely ready |
| Review (5 stages) | Days to a few weeks, depending on queue and how many rounds of fixes are needed |
| Post-approval: first update window | You can update every 2 weeks for your first 2 months, then a 4-week minimum gap |

Build slack into your project timeline for at least one rejection-and-resubmit cycle — it's the norm for a first submission, not a sign something went unusually wrong.

## Best practices

- Set up your Partner Dashboard accounts (see [Partner Dashboard Setup](/publishing/partner-dashboard-setup/)) well before you're actually ready to submit — account verification and store setup can take longer than expected if left to the last minute.
- Budget realistic calendar time for at least one review round-trip, not just the time to build the theme itself.
- Treat "submitted" as the middle of the process, not the end — see [After Approval](/publishing/after-approval/) for the ongoing obligations that start once you're live.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting Partner Dashboard setup the same week you plan to submit**, discovering account verification or store transfer steps take longer than expected.
- **Treating submission as a one-shot event** with no plan for handling a rejection — build the possibility of a fix-and-resubmit cycle into your project timeline from the start.
- **Not reading [After Approval](/publishing/after-approval/) until after approval** — the update cadence and versioning rules affect decisions you should make before you ever submit, like how you structure your first release.

## Quick Reference

- Submission happens through your Partner Dashboard, not a code push.
- Review runs in 5 sequential stages — failing any stage stops the review there.
- Approval isn't the finish line — you're committing to an ongoing update and support cadence.
- Budget for at least one rejection-and-resubmit cycle in your project timeline.
